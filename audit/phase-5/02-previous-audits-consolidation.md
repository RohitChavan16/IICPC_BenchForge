## 1. Traceability & Reconciliation Methodology

Every claim and defect reported in previous audit phases (Phases 1 through 4) was independently cross-referenced against the underlying source code and automated test evidence in `audit/phase-2/tests/`, `audit/phase-3/tests/`, and `audit/phase-4/tests/`. 

Statuses are defined strictly as:
- **Verified**: Directly proven by source inspection and passing reproducible automated tests.
- **Partially Verified**: Conceptually verified or present under specific bounds, but incomplete in edge cases.
- **Unverified**: Marketing or architectural claim with no supporting implementation or test evidence.
- **Failed**: Directly contradicted by source code inspection or failing automated test suites.
- **Blocked**: Cannot be safely evaluated or operated due to an upstream security or architectural dependency.
- **Not Applicable**: Defect rendered moot due to architectural constraints (e.g. multi-tenant interference mooted by global serialization).

## 3. Product-Level Synthesis

Across the 54 individual findings:
1. **Security & Sandbox Isolation (10 findings)**: Fails completely. Running untrusted code is fatal to host infrastructure.
2. **Benchmark Integrity & Anti-Cheat (8 findings)**: Fails. Cheating engines beat genuine engines by 7.7x on the leaderboard.
3. **Metric Accuracy & Precision (7 findings)**: Fails. Sub-millisecond latency rounds to 0.0ms; replay TPS is hardcoded; duration is truncated.
4. **Performance & Scalability (6 findings)**: Heavily constrained. Capped at 1,200 req/s and 1 global concurrent job.
5. **Operational Reliability & Usability (14 findings)**: Intermittent queue starvation, unauthenticated internal ports, broken startup scripts, and compilation errors.
